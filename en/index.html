Frontiers in Neuroinformatics · 2025

Recognising the phases of a movement from EEG

In this study, volunteers grasped, lifted and set down an object. Using electroencephalography (EEG) recordings from the Grasp-and-Lift dataset, we examined how the length of a signal segment, the position of an event within it and added noise affect recognition of the phases of the movement.

Two figures on the research page show the recorded signals and how the classifiers performed under different conditions.

Explore an image at several levels of detail

Change the size of the blocks used to represent a simple shape and compare the visible details. Research context and methods.

Compare three levels of detail

A house with a triangular roof, a rectangular body and a door cut out at the bottom centre.

Compare the three levels of detail in text

The house has a triangular roof, walls and a doorway cut into the centre of the base. At each level, a solid cell lies entirely inside the house. A grey cell covers part of the outline of the house or the edge of the doorway.

  • 8 × 8. Of the 64 cells, 42 contain part of the house, including 28 grey cells. The roof and doorway follow large steps.
  • 16 × 16. Of the 256 cells, 114 contain part of the house, including 44 grey cells. The steps along the roof and the edges of the doorway are finer.
  • 32 × 32. Each cell corresponds to one pixel in the original image. Of the 1,024 cells, 354 are solid and the rest are empty.

Original shape shown.

Each block takes the average shade of the area it covers. This example shows what happens to the outline of an image as its level of detail decreases.

Master’s internships, PhD projects and collaboration

Are you planning a master’s internship, a PhD project or a collaboration in digital accessibility, signal analysis or assistive technologies? Send me an outline of your topic and timeline so that we can discuss supervision and funding possibilities.

Topics, background and documents for an initial enquiry

Topics to discuss

Accessible representations of images and documents; multimodal fatigue analysis; EEG classification and assistive interfaces. The precise topic depends on your background, the available data and the hosting arrangements.

Preparing your enquiry

Master’s internship: state your degree programme, dates, duration and relevant skills in programming, image or signal processing, machine learning or accessibility evaluation. Include a link to your CV and one or two examples of your work.

PhD project: describe your Master’s-level background or equivalent, a research question, proposed methods and timeline. Mention any funding or joint-supervision possibilities. Admission and supervision follow the university’s and doctoral school’s procedures.

Collaboration: introduce your team, scientific objective, the expected contribution of each partner and timeline.

How to contact me

Use the form with the subject “Master’s internship”, “PhD project” or “Collaboration”. Briefly outline your project and add relevant links. The form does not accept attachments: we can then agree how to share any necessary documents. Please do not send sensitive personal data in this first exchange.
