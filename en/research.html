Research

I work on digital accessibility, particularly non-visual access to images and documents. With my colleagues, I also study signal and image processing for assistive technology, machine learning and brain–computer interfaces.

Area 1

Making images and documents more accessible

With Sami Rojbi and Mohamed Salah Gouider, I examined strategies for making web images accessible and the potential of audio and tactile alternatives to complement text descriptions.

Question
How can image content be accessed without relying solely on vision?
Approach
Develop representations at different levels of detail and tactile alternatives suited to different contexts of use.
Contribution and limits
This work explores different ways of representing and interacting with images. The seminar diagrams illustrate the approaches rather than presenting evidence of their effectiveness with users.

Area 2

Studying fatigue with multimodal data

Question
How can visual indicators and physiological measurements be combined to investigate fatigue?
Approach
Combine visual features extracted using computer vision with physiological measurements in a machine-learning approach.
Contribution and limits
I explore this approach through research collaborations and doctoral co-supervision. Area 3

Physical-action classification from EEG signals

I contributed to drafting and revising this collaborative study of electroencephalography (EEG) signals. We examine how temporal context and noise affect the classification of hand movements.

Question
How do temporal context and added noise affect the classification of physical actions from EEG signals?
Approach
Assess the effects of temporal windowing, three label positions and added noise on the performance of a convolutional neural network.
Contribution and limits
The study using the GAL dataset examines the model’s sensitivity to these factors. The network was trained for a single epoch, meaning one pass through the training data. Generalisation to other data remains to be tested, and the results establish neither a universal benefit from synthetic noise nor clinical effectiveness.

Area 4

Tactile maps and non-visual access

With Nizar Bouhlel, I contributed to a tool that supports specialists in preparing tactile maps, presented at ASSETS 2014.

Question
How can the production of tactile maps for blind users be made more efficient?
Approach
Combine image processing, optical character recognition (OCR) and Braille transcription in a semi-automatic tool.
Contribution and limits
A demonstration presented at ASSETS 2014. Adaptation still relies on a tactile-graphics specialist; full system autonomy has not been established.
Read the associated publicationRelated project: E-Tactile

Publication: Nizar Bouhlel and Anis Rojbi, New tools for automating tactile geographic map translation, ACM ASSETS ’14, 2014, pp. 313–314. Read the ACM record and abstract.

Area 5

Multimodal assistive interfaces

My colleagues and I proposed an interface concept combining augmented reality and a brain–computer interface (BCI). It aims to adapt visual, audio and tactile feedback to the user’s needs.

Question
How can an assistive interface adapt to the user’s responses and needs?
Approach
Combine multimodal augmented reality (visual, audio and haptic feedback) with machine-learning analysis of neurophysiological signals.
Contribution and limits
An interface concept and use cases discussed in a conference chapter. Experimental evaluation is needed to establish the effectiveness of a particular implementation.

Publication: User-Driven Intelligent Interface on the Basis of Multimodal Augmented Reality and Brain-Computer Interaction for People with Functional Disabilities, in Advances in Information and Communication Networks (2019), pp. 612–631.
