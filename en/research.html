Research

My research explores accessible images and tactile maps, multimodal fatigue analysis, EEG-based brain–computer interfaces and multimodal assistive interfaces. The sections below combine explanatory diagrams with figures from published work.

Area 1

Making images and documents more accessible

A multi-granular approach transforms visual content into several levels of information that can be used visually, interactively or through tactile representations.

Application. Web and digital document access through alternative, multi-level and tactile representations.
Additional diagrams on granularity

Area 2

Detecting fatigue from multimodal data

The methods combine computer vision, machine learning and physiological measures to extract complementary indicators.

Visual cues

  • facial expressions
  • eye movements
  • behavioural cues associated with fatigue

Physiological measures

  • heart rate
  • blood pressure
  • oxygen saturation

Related work: Parallel Statistical and Machine Learning Methods for Estimation of Physical Load (2018).

Area 4

Tactile maps and non-visual access

This work explores the semi-automated conversion of geographic maps into tactile representations, helping make spatial information accessible to people who are blind.

A demonstration co-authored with Nizar Bouhlel at ACM ASSETS 2014 describes software designed to assist tactile-graphics specialists with this conversion. Image processing, optical character recognition (OCR) and Braille translation are brought together in one tool; the process remains semi-automated and relies on specialist expertise.

Publication: Nizar Bouhlel and Anis Rojbi, New Tools for Automating Tactile Geographic Map Translation, ACM ASSETS ’14, 2014, pp. 313–314. Read the ACM record and abstract.

Area 5

Multimodal assistive interfaces

Multimodal augmented reality and brain–computer interfaces can be combined to design interactions that adapt to the user’s needs.

A 2018 conference chapter co-authored by Anis Rojbi proposes a user-driven accessible-interface concept combining augmented reality and brain–computer interaction. It discusses visual, audio and tactile modalities, alongside machine-learning analysis of neurophysiological responses to support immediate feedback. The chapter presents a concept and use cases; the diagram below does not describe a single product whose effectiveness has been validated.

Publication: User-Driven Intelligent Interface on the Basis of Multimodal Augmented Reality and Brain-Computer Interaction for People with Functional Disabilities, FICC 2018 conference paper, first published online in 2018, pp. 612–631. Read the Springer chapter.
