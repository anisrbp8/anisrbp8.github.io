Research

Area 1

Making images and documents more accessible

With Sami Rojbi and Mohamed Salah Gouider, I studied how people can access the content of a web image when they cannot see it.

Research question
What can words, sound or touch convey about an image?
2023 paper
We reviewed approaches used on the web, where they fall short and how non-speech sounds or tactile representations might complement text descriptions.
Work in progress
I am interested in which image details to retain for tactile exploration. Understanding which levels of detail help will require evaluation with the people who would use them.

Area 3

Physical-action classification from EEG signals

I contributed to writing and revising a collaborative study of hand movements. The team analysed electroencephalography (EEG) recordings to see how well an algorithm could distinguish events such as touching an object for the first time and lifting it.

Research question
Which part of an EEG recording best distinguishes the stages of a hand movement?
Method
The team varied the length of the EEG segments, the position of the labelled event within each segment and the amount of added Gaussian noise. A convolutional neural network classified the events.
Finding and scope
On these data, the model distinguished the actions best when the labelled event fell in the middle of the segment, as figure 8 of the paper shows. The network saw the training data just once. Whether the finding generalises to other recordings remains open.

Area 4

Tactile maps and non-visual access

At ASSETS 2014, Nizar Bouhlel and I presented a tool that helps tactile graphics specialists turn a geographic map into a tactile one.

Research question
Which steps in preparing a tactile map can be automated?
Method
The software simplifies the map image, assigns textures to regions, then recognises place labels and transcribes them into braille.
Role of the specialist
The tool assists tactile graphics specialists, who retain control over how a map is adapted.

Area 5

Multimodal assistive interfaces

In a joint paper for FICC 2018, we proposed combining augmented reality with a brain–computer interface (BCI) to adapt the feedback a person receives.

Research question
How might an interface respond to the way a person reacts to a visual, auditory or tactile cue?
Proposed method
An EEG device records the brain’s electrical activity. A machine-learning model would analyse the signals to help the interface choose what feedback to present.
Scope
The chapter discusses possible uses in education and home care, among other settings. Assessing whether adaptation helps people carry out a task would require testing the interface in use.
