Area 1

Making images and documents more accessible

Question
How can an image be accessed beyond its visual presentation?
Approach
Study representations at several levels of detail and tactile alternatives, taking usage contexts into account.
Contribution and limits
Representation and interaction approaches. The seminar diagrams describe methods; they do not measure effectiveness with users.

Area 2

Detecting fatigue from multimodal data

Question
How can visual and physiological cues be combined to study fatigue?
Approach
Bring together computer vision, physiological measurements and machine learning.
Contribution and limits
An exploratory research direction presented in a seminar. The physical-load paper concerns cardiac analysis; it does not by itself validate the whole illustrated multimodal system.

Area 3

BCI, EEG and physical-action classification

Question
How do temporal context and noise affect action recognition from EEG signals?
Approach
Compare sequence windows, three label positions and added noise using a convolutional neural network.
Contribution and limits
The GAL study examines model sensitivity to these choices. It establishes neither a universal benefit from synthetic noise nor clinical effectiveness; generalisation to other data remains to be tested.

Area 4

Tactile maps and non-visual access

Question
How can maps for blind users be prepared more efficiently?
Approach
Combine image processing, character recognition and braille translation in a semi-automatic tool.
Contribution and limits
A demonstration presented at ASSETS 2014. Adaptation still relies on a tactile-graphics specialist; full system autonomy has not been established.
Read the associated publicationRelated project: E-Tactile

Publication: Nizar Bouhlel and Anis Rojbi, New Tools for Automating Tactile Geographic Map Translation, ACM ASSETS ’14, 2014, pp. 313–314. Read the ACM record and abstract.

Area 5

Multimodal assistive interfaces

Question
How can an assistive interface adapt to the user’s responses and needs?
Approach
Combine visual, auditory and tactile augmented reality, neurophysiological signals and machine learning.
Contribution and limits
An interface concept and use cases discussed in a conference chapter. Experimental evaluation is needed to establish the effectiveness of a particular implementation.
Read the associated publicationDiscuss a collaboration

Publication: User-Driven Intelligent Interface on the Basis of Multimodal Augmented Reality and Brain-Computer Interaction for People with Functional Disabilities, FICC 2018 conference paper, first published online in 2018, pp. 612–631. Read the Springer chapter.
